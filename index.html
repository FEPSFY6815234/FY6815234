Introduction

      The age of the universe is estimated to be around 13.8 billion years old, although the exact age is unknown. The first step towards this estimation is knowing that the atmosphere of stars contains different elements.
      Each specific element, such as oxygen or hydrogen, will absorb unique wavelengths of light. An absortion spectum can then be used which shows which wavelengths of light are missing,
      appearing as a black line in the spectum. However, when you analyse light of stars from distant galaxies, the pattern of black lines looks the same. The only difference being each line is shifted towards 
      the red end of the spectrum. Since waveslength on this end of the spectrum are longer this shows that space itself is expanding and light waves are stretched with it. This is known as a cosmological red shift.
      
      Now that scientists know the universe is expanding, if they can find the rate of expansion, they could estimate the age of the universe. This is because the units of the rate of expansion would be velocity/distance.
      This can be rewritten as distance/(time*distance) which simplifies to 1/time. This means the reciprocal of the rate of expansion states the time taken for the universe to expand to its current size, which is 
      the age of the universe.
